# Where positive expected value could actually come from — research notes

Kwame (S007), 2026-09-27. Task: stop adding strategies that dead-end at "no edge",
analyze what the codebase has already established, and research where genuine positive
expected value could exist on these instruments. Sources are cited inline; nothing here
is asserted from memory alone.

## 1. What the codebase already settles, and what it doesn't

**Digits are algebraically closed — not "hard", closed.** `DESIGN.md` records the measured
payout at all ten barriers, both sides: `payout = 0.95 / p(win)` exactly. So EV per unit is
`p·(0.95/p − 1) + (1−p)·(−1) = −0.05`. The `p` cancels. Eight strategies replayed against
real recorded ticks all settle to "no edge", and that is the *predicted* result, not a
discovery.

The consequence that matters for this memo: **the platform sets payout proportional to
1/p**. It never has to know the true probability better than the market does, because it
re-prices whenever the probability moves. That is a stronger claim than "−5%": it means no
distributional finding about the feed can create a digit edge. Even a non-uniform feed
cannot help, which is exactly why the chi-square check exists to *confirm the door is
closed* rather than to look for a way through it.

Two things the codebase establishes that are *not* dead ends:
- **Staking changes downside, not expectation.** The Martingale wrapper showed ~10× the max
  drawdown at identical EV.
- **The guard is worth real money, measured.** `risk-replay` on 8,000 real Deriv ticks: guard
  intervened in 65% of 40 sessions, worst session −$7.68 guarded vs −$39.23 on the identical
  decisions unguarded; worst-case drawdown $12.97 vs $409.99.
- **The harness is a cheap claim-killer.** Eight sold "tick analysis" claims settled in
  seconds, for free.

**Forex has no algebra.** Whether direction has an edge is a genuine empirical question —
the harness compares the out-of-sample Wilson interval against a random control's *measured*
rate (~43%, because ties count as a loss for either direction; ~14.8% tie rate measured).

## 2. What the literature and the platform say

**Base rate, retail binary/options.** Regulated-market studies put 70–80% of retail binary
options traders at a loss (Broksal, "Why Traders Lose on Binary Options"); MIT Sloan's
research on retail options investors "lose big". The prior against a retail client finding a
durable edge is strong before any strategy is written.

**Short-horizon FX direction.** There *are* documented predictability effects, but they are
the wrong shape for this product:
- Time-of-day effects in FX returns and order flow (Breedon et al., "Intraday Patterns in FX
  Returns and Order Flow") — predictable, but hour-scale and small.
- Intraday time-series momentum across 16 developed markets (Li et al.) — real, and stronger
  on high-volatility days, again at intraday-scale horizons rather than seconds.
- Order flow explains high-frequency FX returns (Berger et al., Fed IFDP 830; Menkhoff et al.,
  BIS WP 405) — the strongest documented driver, and it needs an order book a Deriv binary
  client cannot see. Synthetic indices explicitly *have no order book*.
- Directional predictability is model-dependent and modest (Chung & Hong, "Model-free
  evaluation of directional predictability in FX markets", J. Forecasting 2007).

Net: the effects that exist are hour-scale, small, and/or require data a retail binary client
cannot observe. `forex/harness.py` needs hit rate above the payout breakeven. At a 0.95 ROI,
breakeven is `1/1.95 = 51.28%` of *all* bets (ties included as losses). The three candlestick
strategies measured 0.335–0.435 out-of-sample. Adjusting for the ~14.8% tie rate, their
*decisive* win rates are still only ~39–43%. They are not "no better than random" in the
abstract — they are unprofitable at any plausible payout.

**Deriv's synthetic indices.** All are "generated by a cryptographically secure random number
generator" (deriv.com/markets/derived-indices/synthetic-indices), so the feed is a genuine
random walk and no pattern edge exists on the symmetric ones. Deriv's own FAQ on that page
says so plainly:

> "Synthetic indices, except for Range Break Index, may not be well-suited for technical
> indicators. Since there is no order book ... any noticeable historical patterns are purely
> coincidental. However, Range Break indices fluctuate between support and resistance levels
> before breaking out, so channel analysis and indicators may be effective."

## 3. The one avenue not ruled out by algebra

The same Deriv page lists products whose price process is **deliberately not symmetric**:

- **Range Break** — mean-reverting inside a band, then a regime break (~every 100/200 boundary
  hits); the platform itself says channel analysis "may be effective".
- **Trek** — "the distribution skews toward larger movements in one direction ... 30%
  volatility markets with built-in directional bias".
- **Skew Step** — "asymmetric step sizes and probabilities. With 80% or 90% probabilities for
  small shifts and 10% or 20% for sharp movements".
- **Daily Reset / Drift Switching** — bull/bear regimes, 10–30 minute average durations.

These have *designed-in* drift, skew, or mean-reversion. That is precisely what a directional
strategy needs, and it is the first place in this project where a positive result is not
ruled out by algebra.

**The decisive question is the payout, not the feed.** If the quoted Rise/Fall ROI is a fixed
number (~90–95%) that does **not** scale with the instrument's built-in bias, then a biased feed
plus a fixed payout *is* positive EV, and the harness as built would detect it: a
drift/regime-detecting strategy should beat the random control's hit rate on a biased feed
(the control rolls its own coin, so it does not benefit from drift). If instead the platform
prices payout ∝ 1/p the way it does on digits, the product is still −5% and a trap.

This is testable at zero risk with the existing tooling, and it is the only test in this project
whose success condition is "a verdict other than no-edge".

## 4. The measurement that is actually missing

`forex/harness.py` says it outright:

> "there is no verified payout number to turn a win into a dollar amount yet — real Rise/Fall
> pricing needs Deriv's live quoted terms at entry, which nothing here records."

Verified in code: `api/deriv/ticks.py::tick_record_from_message` builds a `TickRecord` with a
price and digit only — **no payouts**. `TickRecord.payouts` exists and the CryptonicHub DOM
adapter populates it (`browser/cryptonichub.py`), but the Deriv path does not. So on the
platform this project can actually trade, **EV is not computable at all today** — only hit
rate is. The mechanism to fix it already exists unused for this purpose:
`api/deriv/trading.py` issues a `proposal` and reads back `payout`.

Everything in section 3 reduces to one comparison — realized directional frequency vs quoted
ROI. That comparison needs this recording to exist. Filed as **B-2026-09-27-1**.

## 5. Ranked recommendation

1. Let the EUR/USD capture finish; the honest expectation is "no directional edge" (the
   literature above is why). Value: closing the question, not finding money.
2. **Record quoted payouts + contract terms on the Deriv side** (B-2026-09-27-1). Without it,
   no claim about positive EV on Deriv is even measurable.
3. **Point the recorder at the structurally-biased synthetics** — Range Break 100/200 first
   (platform-endorsed), then Trek, Drift Switching, Skew Step. Record ticks *and* payouts,
   replay directional strategies against the random control. This is the one live thread.
4. Only if step 3 shows direction above control *and* the payout under-prices it: that is the
   first genuine positive-EV candidate in the project. Demo-verify before believing it.
5. Otherwise, keep the guard on and treat the tool as a measuring instrument. That is not a
   consolation prize: the guard's measured value (−$7.68 vs −$39.23 worst session) and the
   claim-tester are real, and they need no edge to be worth having.

## 6. Caveats stated honestly

- The platform designs, and can re-price, these products at will. Any edge found is likely to
  be transient and removable; that is a property of the counterparty, not of the strategy.
- A fixed-ROI product on a biased index would be an unusual thing for the platform to offer;
  the more likely outcome is that Deriv has already priced the bias in, and step 3's answer is
  no-edge. The test is worth running precisely because it is the only one that *could* come
  back otherwise.
- "Positive impact" here is most reliably the avoided loss and the measurement, not a durable
  trading profit. The one avenue above is a genuine open question, not a promise.
