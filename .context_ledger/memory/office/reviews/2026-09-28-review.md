# Two trading decks, checked against what this repo can actually measure

You shared two decks — *The Supply & Demand Playbook* (14 slides) and *The
Sneaky Pivot Blueprint* (15 slides) — with no note attached. So the useful
thing to do was read them properly and answer the question they imply: **can
this project test either of them, and should it try?** Short answer: not now,
for three separate reasons that stack. Along the way I found the long-awaited
EUR/USD capture had finally landed, so I ran the existing tooling on it and
got a real verdict on one of the repo's own price-action strategies.

## What the two decks actually claim

**Supply & Demand Playbook.** Wholesale price-action: institutions ("smart
money") leave footprints. You spot a momentum run of 3+ same-coloured candles,
find the last opposite-coloured candle before it, box that candle's high and
low, and extend the box right — that's the zone. You wait for price to wander
back and re-test it; you enter only when the zone is *defended* (a long
lower-wick doji followed by a bullish engulfing candle). Entry at the
engulfing candle's close, stop below the bottom of the box, target the next
zone on the opposite side. Then a layer on top: overlap the zone with a
Fibonacci retracement drawn from swing low to swing high, and when the 61.8%
level lands inside the zone and the engulfing candle sits on it, that's "the
ultimate overlap" — enter there and *risk 2% instead of 1%*. The deck closes
with "Stick to the blueprint. You are just one trade away."

**Sneaky Pivot Blueprint.** Much more concrete — a fully mechanical 15-minute
range system. Draw four horizontal lines before the session: Range High and
Range Low (yesterday's high and low), then swing left for the next extreme
beyond each of those (Swing High and Swing Low). Above the Range High up to
the Swing High is the sell zone; below the Range Low down to the Swing Low is
the buy zone; the middle is "the waiting room — do nothing". Then a 45-minute,
three-candle sequence at whichever boundary price has run to: the first
15 minutes is the anchor that plows to the boundary; the second 15 minutes is
the "sneaky candle" that tests the low and proves intent to buy back up; the
entry is the exact moment price crosses the sneaky candle's high. Stop goes
just under the swing low (the "Guardian Angel"), target is the opposite edge
of the range. Worked examples are two US tickers, AOI (a win) and GGLL (stopped
out), in a fixed morning window.

## The verdict, up front

The two are not equally usable, and it's worth separating them:

- **The Sneaky Pivot deck is nearly a complete specification** and is the only
  one of the two that could ever be turned into code without me inventing
  rules. It still can't be tested here — see reasons 2 and 3.
- **The Supply & Demand deck is not implementable as written**, and that is a
  property of the deck rather than a gap in this repo. Its rules are all
  visual judgements with no threshold attached: how large is a "large" candle,
  which is "the previous opposite-coloured candle" when a run-up follows
  several, what counts as "defense", what is "the biggest seller block". Every
  one of those is a free parameter, and a method with a dozen free parameters
  can be made to look like anything on a chart. Deck 1 also *asserts* rather
  than measures — "highest probability strike zones", "naturally creates high
  Reward-to-Risk ratios", "the big boys always bring the price back" — and its
  evidence is hand-picked charts plus a line from a 26-year professional
  trader. That is a course being sold, not a result being reported.

Deciding to build either one as strategy code today would produce code that
cannot be judged — which is the exact failure this project spent its last
three sessions learning to stop doing.

## Reason 1 — Deck 1's premise has nothing to act on in the feeds we record

"Follow where the institutions are entering" is coherent on a real market. It
is not coherent on `1HZ10V` or `Volatility 10 (1s) Index`, which is what this
project mostly records: those are broker-generated synthetic feeds. There are
no institutional orders in them to leave footprints. EUR/USD *is* a real market
so the premise is at least meaningful there — but that is one underlying, and
the deck's method is demonstrated on US equities in a fixed morning session,
which this repo has no feed for at all.

## Reason 2 — both decks' trade architecture cannot even be expressed here

Both decks trade the same shape: an entry, a stop, and a target. This harness
has no way to say that.

- A decision is `contract` + `stake` + `reason` + `duration` on the digit side
  (`clicktrader/strategies.py:49`) and `direction` + `horizon_ticks` on the
  forex side (`clicktrader/forex/model.py:22`). No entry price, no stop, no
  target, no payoff multiple.
- Grading is a single comparison: `entry_price` at tick *i* against
  `exit_price` at tick *i + horizon* (`clicktrader/forex/harness.py:99-105`),
  and `Signal.wins` is a bare "is exit above entry" test
  (`clicktrader/forex/model.py:32-37`). Everything between those two prices is
  thrown away.

So a strategy whose return comes from **which** of two levels price reached
first is not merely untested — it is inexpressible. Grading it needs a
path-dependent, R-multiple mode, which is an architecture decision, not a
small feature. That gap is worth knowing about independently of these decks:
every method a course teaches is a stop-and-target method, and none of them is
judgeable here today.

## Reason 3 — the data is short by a factor of a few hundred, and that is arithmetic

This is the strongest objection, and it is not a matter of opinion.

The Sneaky Pivot system trades its setup about once a day — one range per day,
one 45-minute sequence. This repo refuses to call anything a verdict below
`MIN_BETS_REPORT = 500` out-of-sample bets (`clicktrader/stats.py:19`), and the
harness splits every capture 50/50, reading only the out-of-sample half. At one
trade a day that is on the order of **a thousand instrument-days — two to four
years of daily 15-minute data** before the method could be judged at all. Deck
1's setups are rarer still, because they need a momentum run *and* a retest
*and* a confirmation candle *and* optionally the 61.8% overlap.

What is actually on disk, measured today:

| recording | ticks | span | wall-clock 15-min bars | calendar days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `forex/live-eurusd.jsonl` | 41,632 | 11.6 h | 47 | 1 (2026-09-25) |
| `forex/live-eurusd-20260928.jsonl` | 60,315 | 16.9 h | 68 | 1 (2026-09-28) |
| `live-deriv.jsonl` | 8,000 | 2.2 h | 9 | 1 |
| `live-cryptonichub.jsonl` | 7,585 | 2.1 h | 9 | 1 |

Two calendar days of EUR/USD in total, and not consecutive — there is a
weekend between them, so "yesterday's range" is available twice, ever. That is
roughly one five-hundredth of what Deck 2 requires.

There is also a mechanical gap in the way, separate from the amount of data:
`CandleBuilder` groups ticks into `bar_size`-*tick* bars, and a `Candle` stores
no timestamp at all (`clicktrader/forex/candles.py:7-9`, `:17-22`). Nothing in
this repo can draw a 15-minute chart, yesterday's high, or a morning session
window. The recordings do carry `ts`, so this is a small fix rather than a dead
end — but it is the prerequisite before Deck 2's inputs can even be
constructed.

## What I measured while I was already in there

The capture that the open thread P-2026-09-27-1 was waiting for has now
happened: the live `record-deriv` process (re-launched by the waiter) has taken
the 2026-09-28 EUR/USD recording past 60,000 ticks, over the ~50k the thread
asked for. I snapshotted it and replayed the three candlestick strategies at
their shipped `bar_size=10`, family-corrected to z=2.39:

| strategy | out-of-sample bets | hit rate | random control | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `engulfing-bar` | 597 | 0.385 (0.339–0.434) | 0.473 (0.425–0.522) | **Worse than the random control** |
| `pin-bar` | 477 | 0.413 (0.360–0.468) | 0.409 (0.357–0.464) | NO VERDICT — 23 bets short of the gate |
| `inside-bar` | 177 | 0.424 (0.339–0.514) | 0.443 (0.360–0.529) | NO VERDICT — fires far too rarely at this bar size |

`engulfing-bar` cleared the 500-bet gate for the first time, and the answer is
negative: 597 bets, and its interval sits entirely below the random control's,
which was measured on the same ticks at nearly the same bet count (600). Its
in-sample rate (0.336) is *worse* than its out-of-sample rate, so this is not a
number that was tuned into looking a particular way.

Because one recording is one session, I re-ran the same three on the
independent 2026-09-25 capture: `engulfing-bar` 0.368 against a control of
0.418, still below, but with only 418 bets it stays under the gate and reads
NO VERDICT. So the direction of the result replicates across two disjoint
sessions; only the newer capture has the power to state it decisively.

**What this does and does not mean.** It does **not** refute "an engulfing
candle at a supply/demand level confirms the zone" — the implemented
`engulfing-bar` fires on the pattern alone, with no zone, level or range
context, on tick-count bars rather than 15-minute bars, and Deck 1's claim is
specifically about the pattern *at a level*. What it does say is that the
naive candlestick-pattern signal on this instrument, taken at face value at its
designed parameters, is not merely unproven: where the data finally became good
enough to judge it, it lands below a coin flip. That is a fair prior to carry
into any price-action deck, and it cost nothing to find because the tooling
already existed.

## What I did *not* do, and why

No product code changed this session — no strategy, no harness change, no
candle work. Two things follow from the above that are worth doing, and neither
is mine to start unilaterally:

1. **Wall-clock bars and previous-day levels.** Small, purely additive, and the
   prerequisite for every session- or timeframe-based method that will arrive
   from course material. Filed as a backlog row rather than built, because
   `candles.py` deliberately documents its tick-count design and replacing that
   with a timestamped-bar concept is the owner's call, not a quiet extension.
2. **A path-dependent (stop/target, R-multiple) grading mode.** This is the
   bigger one and it is an architecture decision, so it is parked as a question
   with the trade-off written down rather than half-built. Note the honest
   caveat: it removes a blocker, it does not unlock these two decks — Deck 2
   would still fail on data quantity, and Deck 1 on specification.

If the question behind the two PDFs was "is price-action worth chasing here",
the engulfing-bar result is the first instalment of the answer, and so far it
is negative.
